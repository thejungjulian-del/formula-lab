Track record of past scans

Every scan is scored automatically after its trading day: bought at the open, sold at the close, after costs. Candidates are compared with buying every scanned stock. This is the real test; the history above was not seen by these trades.

Risks

  • No setup has a proven edge. All 87 setup and holding-period combinations (48 buy, 39 short) failed the out-of-sample test. Most short setups lost money on average: falling stocks tended to bounce. The best won 55–57% of trades, about the same as buying on a random day (stocks drift up over time).
  • Single trades can lose a lot. The worst 2-day trade among these setups lost 25–36%. A 55% win rate still means 45 losers out of 100.
  • Small account math. On $100, an average result of +0.2% is $0.20 per trade. One bad fill or one mistake erases weeks of that.
  • Patterns fade. Published research finds market patterns earn much less after they become known. What worked in 2010–2018 may not work now; the track record above is how you will find out.
  • Data can be late or wrong. Prices come free from Yahoo Finance, which sometimes lags or has gaps. The scan marks itself stale when data is missing the latest session. Never trade on a stale scan.
  • Earnings and news gaps. A 2-day hold can include an earnings report or headline that moves the stock 5–15% overnight. The scan warns when earnings fall inside the hold.
  • The computer has to be on. The scan runs every 30 minutes on weekdays from 6:00 AM to 1:30 PM Pacific. If your Mac is asleep or off, it skips those runs. Check the scan time before acting.
  • Cash-account settlement. Proceeds from a sale settle the next business day. Buying with unsettled money and selling again before it settles can trigger a "good faith violation" and restrictions at your broker.
  • Taxes. Profits on positions held under a year are taxed as ordinary income. Selling at a loss and rebuying within 30 days (wash sale) delays the deduction.
  • Nothing here is guaranteed. It is a measuring tool. Decide what you can afford to lose before trading.
